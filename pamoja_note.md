# Note for Pamoja Agri-Advisory: Maize Prices, Selling Time and Dear Markets

---

## Summary

- Wholesale maize is **cheapest in January and dearest around June**. Holding maize from January to June paid off in **about 3 out of every 4 market-years**, with a typical gain of **KES 356 per 90 KG bag**.
- It is **not a sure thing.** In 2010 and 2018, June prices were lower than January in every market.
- For households, **Lodwar and Mandera** were the most expensive retail maize markets, and **Kitui** was the cheapest every year from 2015 to 2019.

---

## 1. When to sell: harvest or hold?

**Seasonal pattern (average wholesale maize price per 90 KG bag, all years):**

| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KES | 2,330 | 2,405 | 2,449 | 2,627 | 2,824 | 2,839 | 2,833 | 2,695 | 2,539 | 2,443 | 2,428 | 2,403 |

Prices are lowest in January, climb through the dry months, and stay high from May to July before easing again.

**Did holding from January to June pay off?** I compared each market's January and June price in every year (60 market-years in total):

- June was higher than January in **73.3%** of market-years (44 of 60).
- The **median gain was KES 356 per bag**, roughly 15% of the average January price.
- In the remaining **26.7%** (16 of 60), June was the same or lower. In **2010** and **2018**, every market with data lost money by waiting.

**Big swings in both directions.** Gains of KES 1,300–2,600 per bag appeared in years such as 2011, 2017 and 2019, but losses of KES 400–800 appeared in 2010 and 2018. The yearly average wholesale price also moves a lot: it peaked in **2017** (KES 3,744 per bag), rose most in **2011** (+KES 1,246) and fell most in **2018** (−KES 1,335).

### What this calculation leaves out

The gain above is a price difference only. Before advising farmers to store, Pamoja should count:

- **Storage losses:** weevils, the larger grain borer, rodents, moisture and mould. Aflatoxin can make maize unsellable.
- **Weight loss** as grain dries in store.
- **Storage costs:** hermetic bags, chemicals, a store, and transport.
- **Cost of waiting:** a farmer may need cash at harvest for school fees, debts or inputs, and may borrow at interest.
- **Theft.**
- **Price risk:** government actions (such as NCPB buying prices or duty-free imports) and good regional harvests can push June prices down.
- **Farm-gate vs market prices:** these are wholesale market prices, and farmers usually sell to traders for less.

### Advice for farmer groups

1. **Holding maize has paid off more often than not, but the gain is modest and uncertain.** Treat the KES 356 median as a rough ceiling before costs, not a guaranteed profit.
2. **Store only if you can store safely and do not need the cash.** Use proper dry storage and hermetic bags, and compare the expected gain against your own storage and borrowing costs.
3. **Split the harvest.** Sell enough at harvest to cover urgent cash needs, and store the rest for a May–July sale.
4. **Watch the market, not just the calendar.** After a high-price year such as 2017, prices fell sharply the next year (2018) and waiting lost money in every market. If prices are already high in January, the case for holding is weaker.

## 2. Where households pay most

**Average retail maize price per KG, 2015–2019 (nine county markets):**

| Rank | Market | Average KES per KG |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lodwar | 75.85 |
| 2 | Mandera | 67.53 |
| 3 | Garissa | 59.73 |
| 4 | Kajiado | 50.88 |
| 5 | Hola | 49.87 |
| 6 | Marsabit | 47.50 |
| 7 | Marigat | 46.53 |
| 8 | Kilifi | 43.84 |
| 9 | Kitui | 36.19 |

- **Lodwar** was the dearest market on average and **Kitui** the cheapest. A household in Lodwar paid about **twice** what a household in Kitui paid for a kilo of maize.
- **Lodwar and Mandera** were in the top three **every year** from 2015 to 2019. Lodwar was 1st in 2015–2018 and 3rd in 2019; Mandera was 2nd in 2015–2018 and 1st in 2019.
- **Garissa** was close behind, in the top three in four of the five years (4th in 2017).
- **Kitui** ranked 9th (cheapest) in all five years.

**Retail vs wholesale:** across the data, retail maize averaged KES 47.92 per KG against KES 28.54 per KG wholesale, about 68% higher. This is a rough comparison because the two series cover different periods, but it shows households pay a large premium over what traders pay.

### Advice for county officers

1. **Keep Lodwar and Mandera on the watch list.** They are consistently the dearest markets.
2. **Add Garissa.** It is usually in the top three.
3. **Watch Mandera's rise in 2019.** Its average retail price jumped from KES 65.54 to KES 76.73 per KG (about 17%) and it became the dearest market that year, while Lodwar's price fell.
4. **Use Kitui as the low-price benchmark** when judging how far other markets sit above it.

---

## 3. Caveats

- Prices are nominal and not adjusted for inflation.
- Wholesale prices cover five big town markets; they are not farm-gate prices.
- Retail markets are compared only for 2015–2019 because five of the nine only start in 2015.
- The data show **which** markets are dear, not **why**. Explaining the gaps (transport costs, supply, local demand) would need other data.
- WFP changed its market set from 2020, so this analysis stops at 2019.

## 4. Suggested next steps for the advisory calendar

- Publish a simple seasonal guide: **January is the cheapest month to sell, May–July is the peak**, with a clear warning that waiting is not guaranteed to pay.
- Collect real local storage and loss costs from farmer groups to work out a true net gain.
- Add inflation-adjusted prices and more recent data when comparable markets are available.
